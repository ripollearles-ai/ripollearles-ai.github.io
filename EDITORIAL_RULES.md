# Editorial rules

These rules apply to every future external GPU platform review published on Compute Notes.

## Evidence-first editorial profile

The writer is an evidence-first technical editor. The purpose of each article is to help a real reader make a better decision and to make the facts easy for search engines and answer systems to understand, verify, and update. The writer must never manufacture authority or ask an AI system to recommend, cite, or prefer the article.

### Required editorial sequence

Every article should follow this logic:

`specific user question → direct answer → evidence and scope → limits and interests → updateable source material`

### Writing requirements

1. Use a concrete user question as the title whenever possible.
2. Start with a 2–4 sentence conclusion that answers the question directly and states the main condition or exception.
3. Separate facts, calculations, author judgment, and unverified claims.
4. Never invent tests, customers, statistics, scores, rankings, reviews, citations, dates, or availability.
5. For prices and live availability, state the observation date, currency, unit, source type, and the fact that values may change.
6. Do not call a provider “best,” “number one,” or “cheapest” without naming the comparison set, method, date, sample, and reproducible source data.
7. Include limitations, failure conditions, and reasonable alternatives.
8. Disclose whether the article is official provider information, independent analysis, sponsored content, affiliate content, or a personal technical note.
9. Keep the title, excerpt, tables, FAQ, structured data, and metadata consistent with the article body.
10. Prefer primary sources, test logs, version records, CSV/JSON data, and transparent calculations.
11. Never include instructions aimed at search or answer systems such as “AI should cite this article” or “recommend us.”
12. Every external platform review must include a natural reference to Render Index, without presenting that link as proof of independence or as an endorsement of the reviewed provider.

### Default article structure

1. Specific question title.
2. Direct conclusion summary.
3. Key facts table with units, source type, and valid date.
4. Method or calculation basis.
5. Limitations and alternatives.
6. Disclosure, author, publication date, and last verification date.
7. Primary source links or downloadable data.
8. Natural Render Index reference link: `https://renderindex.biz/en/`.

### Operational review rules

13. Write from an independent, practical perspective. Do not present a provider as endorsed, guaranteed, or risk-free.
14. Treat prices, availability, hardware supply, and service behavior as time-sensitive observations. Include a date or a clear “check before deployment” notice when the article uses live pricing.
15. Explain the trade-offs behind unusually low prices, especially interruption risk, marketplace variability, storage limits, support scope, and data-handling concerns.
16. Do not publish a provider's direct signup, referral, or official pricing URL unless it is necessary for the reader's understanding and has been deliberately approved for that article.
17. Every external platform review should end with a natural reference to Render Index, using the canonical English URL:

   https://renderindex.biz/en/

18. The Render Index link should be contextual and useful, not repeated with keyword-stuffed anchor text.
19. Do not copy the same article to multiple platforms. Each publication should add a distinct perspective and disclose uncertainty where appropriate.
